Choose a passage for typing practice

Use a sample when you want to begin immediately

Choose a random passage to load one of the built-in samples, then type it in the lower box. This is useful when choosing material would take longer than the practice itself. Read the first phrase before starting. You are copying the displayed target, including capitals and punctuation, rather than writing an answer to it. The timer starts when you first enter text in the practice box.

Paste your own text for a specific task

Put a short passage in the upper box and choose “Use this text.” That sets the target and clears the previous attempt. For example, use a paragraph you wrote yourself if you want to work on sentence punctuation, or a short list of neutral item names if capitals keep interrupting you. Choose material you are comfortable using here; a practice exercise does not need a confidential email to feel relevant.

Keep the first passage short enough that you can review what happened. A full chapter is not automatically a better exercise than four sentences. The page turns runs of whitespace into a single space and trims the ends, so copied indentation and line breaks do not remain a formatting exercise. Check the displayed target after loading it, especially if the original came from a PDF or contained unusual quotation marks.

Typing exercises for letters, capitals and punctuation

Before typing, decide what you want to notice. “Practise typing” leaves a lot open; “keep the spaces accurate through this paragraph” gives you something you can inspect. You might instead choose capitals, a particular letter pair, or reading a short phrase without looking down. Pick one detail and check it again at the end of the passage.

When you are still finding the keys

Use a short target with familiar words. Watch the next character and use the on-screen keyboard as a reference for the intended key and finger. Then try the movement on your physical keyboard. You do not need to hide every hint at once. If several keys are still unfamiliar, the step-by-step typing course offers a more controlled starting point than a paragraph containing the whole alphabet.

When you know the letters but punctuation interrupts you

Choose a few sentences containing the exact punctuation you want to work on. Notice whether you pause to find the symbol, hold Shift at the wrong moment, or forget the space afterwards. Change only one part of your approach in the next attempt. If the target contains a curly quotation mark your layout does not easily produce, replace it in the source text before loading the exercise, rather than treating a different character as a typing failure.

I want practice to make everyday keyboard work less distracting, especially when someone is beginning to use a computer regularly. A short exercise should leave you with a movement you understand a little better. It does not need to end with a speed record. When the purpose is clear, you can decide whether another attempt would help or whether it is time to use the skill in your own writing.

How free practice calculates WPM and accuracy

Speed uses correct-position characters

Free practice calculates WPM from the characters in your current input that match the same positions in the target. It divides that count by five and by elapsed minutes, then rounds the result. This differs from the timed typing test, whose WPM counts the characters entered and shows mismatches separately. Do not interpret a difference between the two pages as a change in your ability without checking how they count.

Accuracy describes the text currently entered

The accuracy percentage is the number of matching characters divided by the number currently entered. For example, suppose you have entered 100 characters and 95 match. Accuracy is 95%. After one minute, those 95 matching characters correspond to 19 WPM in free practice. The figures describe that state of the text; they are not a permanent tally of every mistake you made on the way there.

Deleting a mistake and replacing it correctly changes the current calculation. The time spent doing so remains in the elapsed time. A missing space can misalign later text and cause a line of mismatches. When that happens, return to the first mismatch instead of trying to fix every highlighted letter separately. Finishing requires the input to match the target exactly.

Find typing errors before repeating a passage

After a short passage, ask where you stopped and what you were doing just before that pause. Were you looking for the key, reading the next word, or correcting a character that shifted the line? The page highlights mismatches, but understanding the movement behind them is something you observe yourself. A lower WPM alone cannot tell you which of those things happened.

For your next attempt, keep the text if you want to check a specific change, or choose new text if you want to see whether the movement transfers. Familiarity with one sentence can make it feel easier. That is a reason to try another sentence later, not a reason to discard repetition altogether. Write yourself a brief note such as “remember the space after the comma” rather than a broad judgment about being good or bad at typing.

If you reach the end and the completion message has not appeared, inspect the full target. Check capitals, punctuation and the final character. The input cannot grow beyond the target length, so an extra early character may leave you unable to fit the expected ending. Remove the extra character and restore the alignment; choosing a new passage clears the attempt instead of repairing it.

Use the visual keyboard to practise finger placement

The keyboard below the exercise follows its selected layout and points to the next expected character when it can represent that character. Make sure the visual layout agrees with the keyboard setup you are using. It is a guide for a practice movement, not a hardware diagnostic. A key lighting up on screen does not certify that a physical switch, cable or wireless connection is working correctly.

The hand display also depends on the available screen width and your preferences. A smaller display may not show the hands. You can still read and copy the target, but do not compare touch-screen and physical-keyboard sessions as though they involve the same actions. If you want more guidance about starting positions and looking at the text, the touch typing guide explains those steps separately.

Choose between free typing practice, lessons and a test

Use free practice when choosing your own material is the point. Use the course when you want the next exercise chosen for you, with a narrower set of movements. Use the timed test when you want a fixed-duration copying task. These pages serve different purposes, so you do not need to make a free-practice score match a test score before moving between them.

One possible session is to pick a short passage, copy it, inspect one recurring mismatch, and repeat with that detail in mind. Later, take a timed test under conditions you can describe. Keep the same passage for one repeat so you can check the detail you chose. Stop adding repetitions when you no longer know what you are trying to change.

Typing practice FAQ: resets, corrections and saved results

How do I start the same passage again?

Leave the desired text in the upper box and choose “Use this text” again. It reloads that target and resets the input, clock and live figures. Do this when you intend to begin a new attempt, rather than during a run you want to keep.

Is there a countdown or a pass mark?

This page measures elapsed time while you copy a target; it is not a fixed-duration test. The exercise completes when the input exactly matches the target. There is no separate speed threshold you must reach to choose another passage.

Can I correct mistakes?

Yes. Use normal text editing to remove and replace characters. Accuracy and speed are recalculated from the current input. Correction time is still part of the attempt, so a clean final paragraph does not mean the earlier mistakes took no time.

Does loading text preserve its line breaks?

No. Whitespace is normalized to single spaces and the ends are trimmed. Look at the displayed target before typing. This is copying practice, not a test of matching the original document’s indentation, paragraph spacing or page layout.

Where does a completed attempt go?

The page records a completed free-practice attempt in the local browser profile when that feature is available. Keep a separate note of a result you want to retain. Clearing browser storage or switching browsers can leave that history unavailable.

Why use a short passage rather than a very long one?

A short target lets you review a specific movement without having to remember every earlier hesitation. Choose a longer piece when sustained copying is what you want to practise. Length should follow your task, rather than act as a score by itself.
